The ADA web accessibility deadline for government websites just moved. Moved doesn't mean gone.

Back in 2024, the DOJ finalized a rule requiring state and local government websites and mobile apps to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA standards under ADA Title II. Then in April 2026, DOJ extended those deadlines by a year. Large public entities, meaning a population of 50,000 or more, now have until April 26, 2027. Smaller entities and special districts have until April 26, 2028.

If you're a nonprofit or a government contractor, here's what actually changed, and what didn't.

## It's not just your main website

This rule covers a lot more ground than a homepage refresh. It applies to mobile apps, online forms, PDFs, and any third-party or vendor-managed content posted on a government's behalf. So if a prime contractor builds a portal or a form on an agency's site, that content is in scope too. And here's the part that surprises people: responsibility stays with the public entity, even when a vendor built it.

## The extension buys time, not an exemption

Private ADA lawsuits don't run on DOJ's timeline. Thousands get filed every year, deadline or no deadline. By some counts, over 8,600 were filed in 2025 alone. Waiting until the compliance date gets closer just compresses the work into a shorter window.

## You can't really bolt accessibility on later

Retrofitting an existing site for WCAG 2.1 AA compliance is a much heavier lift than building it in from the start. Things like proper heading structure, alt text, color contrast, keyboard navigation, and form labeling work best when they're part of the architecture from day one. Trying to patch them in after the fact almost always costs more time and money.

## For nonprofits, this is a mission question

If your website is how people find services, apply for programs, or reach your team, an inaccessible site becomes a barrier to the exact people your organization exists to serve. Compliance and mission alignment point in the same direction here, which makes this an easier case to prioritize than most technical fixes.

## For government contractors, this is a procurement question

Agencies are already being told to require WCAG 2.1 AA conformance in every vendor contract. That means demonstrating accessibility competence is quickly becoming part of what makes a delivery partner credible, not just a checkbox on a capability statement.

## What to do with the extra time you now have

If your organization has a website refresh planned in the next year or two, this is the moment to build accessibility into that process from the start rather than treating it as a separate audit later. The extension bought you time. It didn't remove the work.
